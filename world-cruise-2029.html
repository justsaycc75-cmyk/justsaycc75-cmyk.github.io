Grand Princess · 2029

Our trip of a lifetime

From Sydney Harbour to the South Pacific, through the Panama Canal and across the Atlantic, around Europe and back home by way of the Americas and Tahiti. This is the journey we are looking forward to, one stretch of ocean at a time.

23 May – 29 September 2029129 nights13 illustrated chaptersSydney to Sydney

We plan to take the voyage at our own pace: enjoy the sea days, watch the approaches from the deck and choose the port outings that suit us. These illustrations show the journey in broad strokes. They are artistic maps, and the itinerary and port times may change. Back to Holidays on the horizon.

Chapter 01

Australia & South Pacific

23 May – 4 June 2029

Sydney Harbour begins the voyage with the Opera House and Harbour Bridge in view. Brisbane follows on the winding Brisbane River, with its waterfront precincts and subtropical gardens. Across the Coral Sea, Nouméa blends French and Melanesian influences beside broad bays; tiny Mystery Island is known for its reef and clear water. Suva is Fiji’s busy capital, while Nukuʻalofa is the centre of Tonga’s island kingdom. Rarotonga closes this first chapter with a volcanic green interior ringed by a lagoon and coastal villages.

Chapter 02

Pacific crossing & Caribbean

4 – 24 June 2029

The long Pacific crossing links Rarotonga with Puntarenas on Costa Rica’s Gulf of Nicoya, a coastal gateway to the country’s forests and wildlife. Fuerte Amador sits at the Pacific entrance to the Panama Canal, with Panama City across the bay. The canal passage carries ships through locks and Gatún Lake to the Caribbean. Cartagena brings a walled colonial old town and formidable Spanish fortifications; Oranjestad has the colourful streets of Aruba’s capital. Bridgetown, Barbados, adds a historic harbour before the route turns towards the Atlantic.

Chapter 03

Atlantic & western Mediterranean

24 June – 13 July 2029

Six sea days after Barbados lead to Funchal on Madeira, a port framed by steep green hills, gardens and streets descending to the harbour. Santa Cruz de Tenerife is the main city of Tenerife in the Canary Islands. Cádiz occupies a peninsula on Spain’s Atlantic coast, while Gibraltar’s famous Rock stands beside the narrow strait between Europe and Africa. Inside the Mediterranean, Barcelona offers striking architecture and a lively waterfront; Cartagena in Spain has Roman remains and a long naval history.

Chapter 04

Western Mediterranean

5 – 10 July 2029

Gibraltar marks the entrance to a compact run of Mediterranean ports. Ajaccio, Corsica’s capital, faces a sheltered gulf and has close links with Napoleon Bonaparte. Livorno is a working Tuscan port with canals and fortresses as well as connections to Pisa and Florence. On Elba, Portoferraio’s harbour is watched over by old fortifications and hillside streets. Civitavecchia is the sea gateway to Rome, whose ancient sites, squares and churches lie inland from the cruise terminal.

Chapter 05

Rome to Ireland

10 – 22 July 2029

Civitavecchia begins a route that returns to Barcelona’s modernist streets and Cartagena’s maritime heritage before reaching Lisbon. Portugal’s capital rises in layers above the Tagus River, with tiled buildings, steep lanes and broad river views. Farther north, Galway sits where the River Corrib meets the Atlantic, and Killybegs is a major fishing harbour on Donegal’s coast. Greenock opens onto the Firth of Clyde in Scotland. Dublin’s River Liffey and Cork’s branching River Lee give the Irish city calls quite different settings.

Chapter 06

Ireland, Scotland & northern Europe

19 – 25 July 2029

Galway’s old centre and the fishing fleet at Killybegs give this section a strong Atlantic character. Greenock stretches along the Clyde, with views towards the hills across the water. Dublin is built around the Liffey, with Georgian streets and a long literary history; Cork occupies channels and islands in the River Lee. The route then reaches Dover, where the white chalk cliffs mark the English Channel, and Zeebrugge, Belgium’s North Sea port and the gateway to Bruges and its medieval canals.

Chapter 07

Northern Europe & Norway

25 July – 3 August 2029

Amsterdam’s canal ring begins a northward journey through the Netherlands, Denmark and Norway. Skagen sits at the tip of the Jutland peninsula, while Copenhagen combines a historic harbour with palaces and lively streets. Kristiansand and Stavanger introduce Norway’s southern coast; Stavanger’s old wooden houses rise above its harbour. Nordfjord is a long inlet framed by mountains, and Skjolden lies deep inside the Sognefjord system. The sailing through these fjords is as much a part of the story as the towns at their ends.

Chapter 08

Iceland & North Atlantic

4 – 13 August 2029

Runavík lies on Eysturoy in the Faroe Islands, among steep islands and working harbours. Reykjavík brings Iceland’s capital, geothermal culture and a waterfront facing Faxaflói Bay. Ísafjörður sits deep in the Westfjords, where mountains close around the town and its harbour. Across the North Atlantic, Corner Brook occupies a sheltered part of western Newfoundland, while Gaspé stands on Québec’s peninsula near the Gulf of St. Lawrence. This chapter moves from Nordic volcanic and fjord landscapes to the wooded coast of eastern Canada.

Chapter 09

Canadian Maritimes

13 – 19 August 2029

Gaspé introduces the Gulf of St. Lawrence before the route reaches Charlottetown on Prince Edward Island, a small capital with historic streets and a waterfront. The Îles de la Madeleine are a chain of islands known for dunes, beaches and red sandstone cliffs. Sydney on Cape Breton Island has strong maritime and Celtic connections. Halifax offers a long harbourfront, restored buildings and museums tied to Atlantic travel. Saint John, New Brunswick, sits on the Bay of Fundy, famous for its exceptionally large tides.

Chapter 10

USA, Caribbean & Panama Canal

20 – 28 August 2029

New York brings the Hudson River, harbour skyline and dense neighbourhoods of a city built by arrivals from around the world. Miami is a different American waterfront: subtropical, with strong Latin American influences and the Art Deco streets of nearby Miami Beach. George Town on Grand Cayman is the capital of an island surrounded by reefs and clear Caribbean water. The second Panama Canal transit then crosses the isthmus back towards the Pacific, passing locks, Gatún Lake and the great flow of international shipping.

Chapter 11

South America & Easter Island

29 August – 9 September 2029

Manta is a working port on Ecuador’s Pacific coast, with beaches and a strong fishing tradition. Callao is the port for Lima, whose historic centre preserves churches and buildings from the Spanish colonial era. Farther south, the San Martín port serves the Paracas coast near Pisco, an area of desert meeting the sea. A long stretch of open Pacific follows before Rapa Nui, or Easter Island, appears. Its moai and ceremonial sites speak to a remarkable Polynesian history; getting ashore depends on the conditions.

Chapter 12

Easter Island to Tahiti

9 – 16 September 2029

Rapa Nui is among the most remote inhabited islands on earth, with its famous moai standing across a volcanic landscape. The route continues to Pitcairn, whose small community is closely associated with the descendants of the Bounty mutineers; the call is usually experienced from the ship because landing can be difficult. Papeete is Tahiti’s busy capital, with markets and a waterfront shaped by French Polynesian life. Nearby Moorea is known for jagged green peaks, bays and a lagoon that wraps around the island.

Chapter 13

Homeward Pacific to Sydney

16 – 29 September 2029

The homeward route leaves French Polynesia for a long Pacific crossing to Auckland, New Zealand’s harbour city between the Waitematā and Manukau waters. Tauranga sits on the Bay of Plenty and is the port for the Rotorua region. Norfolk Island follows, with a distinctive mix of Pacific scenery and convict-era history. The final sea days bring the ship back to Sydney on 29 September, closing a circle that began in the same harbour 129 nights earlier.

More of the plans and port stories will take shape as the departure gets closer.
