Grand Princess · 2029

Our trip of a lifetime

From Sydney Harbour to the South Pacific, through the Panama Canal and across the Atlantic, around Europe and back home by way of the Americas and Tahiti. This is the journey we are looking forward to, one stretch of ocean at a time.

23 May – 29 September 2029129 nights13 illustrated chaptersSydney to Sydney

We plan to take the voyage at our own pace: enjoy the sea days, watch the approaches from the deck and choose the port outings that suit us. These illustrations show the journey in broad strokes. They are artistic maps, and the itinerary and port times may change. Back to Holidays on the horizon.

Chapter 01

Australia & South Pacific

23 May – 4 June 2029

We leave Sydney, call at Brisbane and head into the warm water of the South Pacific. Nouméa, Mystery Island, Suva, Nukuʻalofa and Rarotonga make this first leg feel like a holiday in its own right. There is plenty of time to settle into shipboard life between the island days.

Chapter 02

Pacific crossing & Caribbean

4 – 24 June 2029

A run of quiet sea days takes us across the Pacific to Costa Rica and Panama. The Canal passage is one to watch from the deck; on the other side, Cartagena, Aruba and Barbados bring a very different coastline before the Atlantic crossing begins.

Chapter 03

Atlantic & western Mediterranean

24 June – 13 July 2029

After Barbados, the ocean takes centre stage. Madeira and Tenerife bring us back to land, followed by Cádiz and Gibraltar. Barcelona and Cartagena in Spain lead us into the Mediterranean, where even an easy walk from the port can make a memorable day.

Chapter 04

Western Mediterranean

5 – 10 July 2029

Gibraltar gives way to Corsica, the Tuscan gateway of Livorno, Elba and Civitavecchia for Rome. This short stretch is packed with places that could fill several separate holidays, so the pleasure will be in choosing a few unhurried moments rather than trying to do everything.

Chapter 05

Rome to Ireland

10 – 22 July 2029

From Rome the itinerary loops back past Barcelona and Cartagena, then Lisbon opens the way to the Atlantic coast. Galway and Killybegs begin a run around Ireland, with Greenock on the Clyde, Dublin and Cork to follow. It is a lovely change from Mediterranean heat to harbours and old streets.

Chapter 06

Ireland, Scotland & northern Europe

19 – 25 July 2029

Several ports deserve a closer look: the fishing harbour at Killybegs, Greenock and the Clyde, Dublin and Cork. Then we round Britain for Dover and Zeebrugge, a gateway to Bruges. We can decide on each morning whether to explore farther or simply enjoy the port town at our own pace.

Chapter 07

Northern Europe & Norway

25 July – 3 August 2029

Amsterdam, Skagen and Copenhagen lead into Norway. Kristiansand and Stavanger offer town days, while Nordfjord, Skjolden and Sognefjord promise the kind of scenery we can enjoy without leaving the ship. This is a stretch for keeping a camera close and a warm layer handy.

Chapter 08

Iceland & North Atlantic

4 – 13 August 2029

Runavík in the Faroe Islands marks the move into the North Atlantic. Reykjavík and Ísafjörður follow, then sea days carry us to Corner Brook in Newfoundland and Gaspé in Québec. The scenery shifts from volcanic landscapes and fjords to Canada's rugged eastern shores.

Chapter 09

Canadian Maritimes

13 – 19 August 2029

The Canadian coast deserves a slower chapter of its own. Gaspé, Charlottetown, the Magdalen Islands, Sydney on Cape Breton, Halifax and Saint John make a chain of waterfront towns and maritime stories. The Bay of Fundy is a fitting finale before we turn south.

Chapter 10

USA, Caribbean & Panama Canal

20 – 28 August 2029

New York and Miami bring two very different city days. After Grand Cayman, the route returns to the Panama Canal for a second passage, this time taking us back towards the Pacific. It will be fascinating to see the locks and the traffic from the opposite direction.

Chapter 11

South America & Easter Island

29 August – 9 September 2029

On the Pacific side, Manta, Callao for Lima and Pisco's Paracas coast mark our last mainland calls. The sea days that follow lead to Rapa Nui, also known as Easter Island. Its call is weather dependent, so even a scenic view from the ship would be a memorable part of the journey.

Chapter 12

Easter Island to Tahiti

9 – 16 September 2029

The route traces a long line through remote Pacific islands: Easter Island, Pitcairn, Papeete and Moorea. Shore access at the first two can depend on sea conditions, and Pitcairn may be viewed from the ship. Tahiti and Moorea then bring a lush change of scene for the homeward leg.

Chapter 13

Homeward Pacific to Sydney

16 – 29 September 2029

After Moorea, sea days lead back across the Pacific to Auckland and Tauranga, then Norfolk Island. Sydney Harbour comes into view again on 29 September. By then the ship, the sea days and the changing view from the deck will have been part of our lives for more than four months.

More of the plans and port stories will take shape as the departure gets closer.
