Frequency library

What each channel is for, what to listen for, and where to try it. Utility services can be silent for long periods.

HFGCS

11,175 kHz USB

HFGCS primary

US High Frequency Global Communications System. Publicly monitored; activity is intermittent.

USED FOR

Long-range command-and-control contact between US military stations and aircraft worldwide.

LISTEN FOR

Coded Emergency Action Messages, callsigns, radio checks and occasional aircraft requests.

Twente →South Australia →NA5B USA →
HFGCS

8,992 kHz USB

Lower HFGCS companion

Useful when 11 MHz is not carrying or the radio path has moved into darkness.

USED FOR

Lower-frequency coverage for the same worldwide HFGCS network.

LISTEN FOR

HFGCS callsigns, coded messages and signal checks.

Twente →South Australia →NA5B USA →
HFGCS

15,016 kHz USB

HFGCS high band

Daylight-side HFGCS channel when the path supports 15 MHz.

USED FOR

Carries the worldwide HFGCS network on higher HF paths.

LISTEN FOR

Brief coded messages and aircraft or ground-station calls.

Twente →South Australia →NA5B USA →
HFGCS

4,724 kHz USB

HFGCS low band

Night-path HFGCS channel, often the better late-night choice.

USED FOR

Provides HFGCS coverage on a lower band that can travel better through fully dark paths.

LISTEN FOR

Brief coded voice traffic, callsigns and checks amid more night-time static.
